A one-person business has one bookkeeper, and it's you. Nobody else will notice a deposit you never invoiced, a receipt that never made it off the dashboard of the truck, or a fourth-quarter estimate due January 15. The good news is that a solopreneur's books are small. They don't need much. They need the same few things done every month.

I build Poof, so read the second half of this with that in mind. The first half applies whatever software you use, including a spreadsheet.

## The five things a solopreneur's books have to do

**1. Keep business money apart.** One business checking account and one business card, used for nothing personal. When you pay yourself, it is an owner's draw, not an expense, and it never touches your profit. Mixing the two is what turns a CPA's January into a cleanup, and their bill with it.

**2. Tie every deposit to whoever paid it.** A $2,400 deposit is income from a client, a refund from a supplier, or a transfer from your savings. Only one of those is taxable. If each payment matches an invoice, the question never comes up.

**3. Put every expense on its Schedule C line, with the receipt.** Your return groups expenses into about twenty lines: advertising, car and truck expenses, supplies, meals, rent, and so on. Booking expenses to those lines through the year means the return is a printout in January, not a project. Keep the receipt for anything you'd have to explain. If you drive for work, log the miles as you go; a log rebuilt from memory in March is hard to defend.

**4. Know what you owe so far, and pay it in four parts.** No employer withholds tax for you. Self-employment tax is about 14% of profit before income tax starts. For 2026, estimates are due April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. The [quarterly estimated taxes](/blog/quarterly-estimated-taxes) guide has the safe-harbor amounts, and the [set-aside calculator](/tax-set-aside-calculator) works out how much of each month's profit to put away.

**5. Send 1099s to the people you paid.** If you paid a person or an unincorporated business $2,000 or more for services in 2026, by check, ACH, cash or Zelle, they get a 1099 by February 1, 2027. The threshold was $600 until this year. [Who gets a 1099](/blog/who-gets-a-1099) covers the edge cases.

That's it. Budgets, forecasts and a balance sheet you can read are worth having once those five run without effort. Until then they're extra.

## What that costs in QuickBooks

QuickBooks has a free plan now, and for a solopreneur it is the obvious first look. As of November 2, 2026, Intuit's pages list QuickBooks Free at $0 with 1 user and no accountant access, 2 invoices and 1 estimate a month (invoices are unlimited if you qualify for and turn on QuickBooks Payments), 2 receipts a month, 5 mileage trips, 1 connected bank account and 3 reports.

A freelancer with four clients outgrows that in the first month, unless those clients pay through QuickBooks Payments. The next plans are Lite at $20 a month and Simple Start at $38. ## Taxes, where a solopreneur usually gets hurt

Most of the trouble a one-person business has with the IRS isn't the return. It's April arriving with no money set aside, or a penalty for estimates that were never paid. Poof's Books → Taxes screen is built for that, and it is free:

- **The reserve.** Two numbers side by side on the dashboard: the tax this year's profit has built up so far, and what's in the bank account you named as your tax savings. The rate starts at 25%, labelled a starting guess, and you or your CPA can change it. When the reserve falls behind, a monthly reminder says by how much.
- **The four estimates.** The safe-harbor amount (the smaller of 90% of this year's tax and 100% of last year's, or 110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000), on the real due dates, paid by you on a 1040-ES. An IRS payment in the bank feed is offered for the right quarter for you to confirm; one paid from a personal account, you type in.
- **The year on Schedule C lines.** Every account mapped once to the line it belongs on and carried forward, tied to the books' net income.
- **A package for your CPA.** One PDF in January with the mapped year, the questions they'll ask with your numbers next to them (meals, vehicles, owner draws, 1099s), and the balance sheet at December 31.

Poof prepares and reminds. It never files your return, never pays the IRS and never moves money. The transfer to your tax account, and the estimate itself, are yours to make.
