LazyBooks launched on November 9, 2026. Chris Koerner announced it with the line "QuickBooks is the enemy of small business owners," a price of $19 a month, and 60 days free. If you are paying Intuit $38 to $140 a month, that will get your attention, and it should. I build Poof, which competes with both, so read this knowing that. Here is an honest look at what LazyBooks is, how it compares with QuickBooks, and where Poof fits.

Everything below about LazyBooks comes from its own website, terms and privacy policy, from the launch post, and from an account I opened to test it, all on November 10, 2026. It is a day old. It will change.

## What LazyBooks is

LazyBooks is simple bookkeeping with AI doing the sorting. You connect your bank and cards through Plaid and it brings in up to two years of history. It suggests a category for each transaction, you approve them from a short list, and rules handle the repeats. Its site says "nothing is final until you approve it," and that everything also works by hand.

Underneath, it keeps double-entry books. You reconcile to each bank statement one month at a time. There are three reports: Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet and Cash Flow. Invoices go out with a payment link, customers pay by card or bank debit through Stripe, and each payout is matched to its bank deposit. LazyBooks adds no fee of its own: you pay Stripe's standard 2.9% plus 30 cents on cards, or 0.8% capped at $5 on bank debits. There is a catalog of your products and services, a customer list, and a starter chart of 42 accounts you can rename or add to. The home page is a to-do list: transactions to approve, invoices overdue. You can invite teammates.

The feature it leads with is "Bring your own AI." You paste LazyBooks' server address into Claude, ChatGPT or another AI app, pick which businesses it can see, and choose read-only or full access. With full access, the outside AI can approve transactions and send invoice reminders for you. There is an assistant inside LazyBooks too, and that one shows you a card to approve before it makes a rule.

## What it doesn't have yet

LazyBooks' site lists six features, and the menu inside the app matches it: transactions, reconciliation, reports, accounts, invoices, a catalog, customers and payments. These are not on the site or in the menu as of November 10, 2026:

- Bills, purchase orders and what you owe vendors
- Receipts
- Estimates or quotes
- 1099s and payroll
- Sales tax tracking or reports (the starter accounts include Sales Tax Payable, but nothing in the menu works it out)
- Job costing or projects
- A month-end close or locked periods
- A pricing page on the website

One more thing to know before you sign up: the 60 free days start after you enter a card.

That is not a criticism. It is a new product with a deliberately short list, and a short list is the point. It does mean a business with vendors to pay, contractors to file 1099s for, or jobs to cost should ask before moving its books.

## LazyBooks vs QuickBooks

| | LazyBooks | QuickBooks Online |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $19/mo after 60 days free, per the launch post; card required to start | Free (limited), Lite $20, Simple Start $38, Essentials $85, Plus $140 per month |
| Bank feeds | Plaid, up to two years of history | Any bank or card; one account on Free |
| Categorization | AI suggests, you approve from a list; rules for repeats | Bank feed with suggestions and bank rules |
| Reports | Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow | Three on Free; more on paid plans |
| Invoicing | Card and bank-debit payments through Stripe, no added fee | Built in; 2 a month on Free |
| Bills, inventory, projects, budgets | Not mentioned | On the higher plans |
| Outside AI apps | Claude, ChatGPT and others, read-only or full access | Intuit's own assistant |
| Accountants | Standard double-entry reports; teammates by invite | QuickBooks Online Accountant, which most CPAs already use |
| Track record | Launched November 9, 2026 | Two decades and a large app marketplace |

One note on the headline number. The launch post compares $19 with QuickBooks at $38. That is Simple Start. QuickBooks also sells Lite at $20 and has a free plan, though the free plan stops at 2 invoices a month and one bank account. Against Lite, the saving is a dollar; against Plus, it is $121.

**Pick LazyBooks** if you run a simple business, mostly need transactions sorted and a clean P&L for your accountant, send a few invoices, and like the idea of doing your books from Claude or ChatGPT.

**Pick QuickBooks** if your CPA works only in QuickBooks, you need inventory, projects or payroll in one place, or you depend on apps that plug into it. For why its price keeps rising, see [why QuickBooks is so expensive](/blog/quickbooks-too-expensive-alternatives).

## Where Poof fits

Poof starts from the same complaint about QuickBooks and makes three different choices.

**The books are free.** Not for 60 days. Invoices and quotes, bills, purchase orders, every report, reconciliation, the month-end close, 1099 prep, sales tax tracking and two bank feeds cost nothing, with no card. What costs money is the AI doing the work: Poof Agent sorts your transactions overnight, reads every receipt and bill, and drafts your reminders, for $79 a month after 30 days free. A new business that wants a person instead can ask for a bookkeeper who works in Poof.

Be clear-eyed about that trade. If what you want is AI sorting your transactions for the least money, LazyBooks includes it at $19 and Poof charges $79. If what you want is the books themselves for the least money, Poof is $0.

**More of the back office.** Poof handles the bills you owe as well as the invoices you send, files 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC with the IRS (the first three forms a year are free), tracks sales tax, posts payroll from your provider's reports, and closes the month on a 19-task checklist where the books prove 15 of the tasks themselves. For service shops it tracks profit per job and runs the dispatch board.

**An outside AI can read, not write.** This is the real difference in philosophy. In LazyBooks' own demo, the owner tells an AI app to approve transactions and it approves 15 of them. In Poof, Claude and ChatGPT can answer anything from your books, but they cannot post an entry, send an invoice or approve a thing. Every entry Poof's own AI proposes waits on a card with the evidence it found, and a person approves it.

For a solo owner with simple books, LazyBooks' way is faster and may be exactly right. For anyone who has had to explain a number to a lender, a partner or the IRS, "an AI approved what an AI suggested" is a hard sentence to say out loud.

## What I would do

If your business is you, a bank account and a card, and the thing you hate is sorting transactions, try LazyBooks. Sixty days is long enough to find out, and $19 is a fair price for not doing it yourself. Put the end date on your calendar, since your card is on file.

Before you move, ask three questions of any new books, mine included. Can I get everything out if I leave? Who approved each entry, and can I see why? What happens in January when contractors need 1099s?
