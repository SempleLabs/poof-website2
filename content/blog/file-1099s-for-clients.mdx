A bookkeeper or accountant can file every client's 1099s through IRIS with one TCC: apply once, choose the Transmitter role, and use that code for every client. FIRE closes on November 19, 2026, so a FIRE TCC won't file a single 2026 form. If you don't have an IRIS transmitter TCC yet, apply this month; the IRS says to allow up to 45 days. The forms are due February 1, 2027.

The filing itself is the easy part. The hard part is the three weeks before it: W-9s missing across a dozen clients, owners who haven't looked at the list, and payments that only show up in the bank. Here is how IRIS works for a firm, and how to make January shorter.

## IRIS for a firm: Issuer or Transmitter

When you apply for an IRIS TCC, you pick one role. You can't pick both on one application.

| Role | Who it's for | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | A business filing only its own forms | The issuer's EIN must match the EIN on the TCC application |
| Transmitter | A person filing for their own business and other businesses | One TCC covers every client you file for |

The IRS encourages transmitters who file for several businesses to submit one application, choose the Transmitter role, and use that TCC for all of them. A few details that matter when you have a client list:

- **One CSV, several clients.** A transmitter's CSV file can contain multiple issuers. Each file has to be one tax year and one form type, so clients' 1099-NECs go in one file and their 1099-MISCs in another.
- **Upload limits.** Up to 100 forms per submission; CSV files hold up to 250 records.
- **Saved issuers.** The portal's Issuer Management screen saves up to 25 frequent issuers for keyed entry. That limits saved profiles, not the number of clients you can file for.
- **Recipient copies are still yours.** IRIS generates PDF copies for each contractor, but it doesn't email or mail them. For a firm with 15 clients, that can be a few hundred envelopes or emails.
- **The application needs an EIN** and a responsible official who signs with a PIN. Authorized delegates can be added.

The IRS's IRIS publications don't require a separate client authorization form to transmit 1099s. Cover 1099 filing in your engagement letter, and get each owner's sign-off on the amounts before you file, because the owner is the one whose name is on the form.

## The January crunch

Every firm that files 1099s knows the shape of January:

1. **Find out who was paid.** Some clients keep the books with you all year; others send a spreadsheet in January. Payments by check, ACH, cash, and Zelle count toward the $2,000 line. Card and PayPal payments are on the processor's 1099-K and come out.
2. **Chase W-9s.** Across a dozen clients, that is dozens of contractors, many of whom stopped answering email in October. The [W-9 guide](/blog/collect-w9-from-contractors) has the request emails.
3. **Total and check.** Who crossed $2,000 for 2026, which box, and whether any December checks cleared in January. The [2026 threshold guide](/blog/1099-threshold-2026) covers the rules that changed.
4. **Get the owner's sign-off.** Usually by email, usually late.
5. **File and send copies.** By February 1.

Steps 2 and 4 are where the days go. Anything that starts the W-9 chase in November and makes the owner's sign-off one click saves more time than a cheaper per-form price.
